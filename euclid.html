Euclid

Grade justification, criterion by criterion

What it produces

Euclid produces a structured report showing how each evaluation criterion leads to the final grade.

Each criterion is assessed explicitly.
Each step of the reasoning is visible.
The grade is not inferred - it is built.

What makes it different

Most tools generate feedback.
Euclid produces a report that mirrors how a reviewer justifies a grade.
It makes the evaluation logic explicit, stable, and repeatable.

Illustration: Structural and argumentative analysis of a long-form submission.

Why it matters

A grade without structured justification is fragile.
Euclid turns grading into a documented decision.

What it costs not to have it

  • grades are harder to defend
  • evaluators diverge
  • review processes slow down
