Applied Competency Assessment

The value of a degree depends not only on the acquisition of theoretical knowledge but also on the ability to apply it in real professional situations. Academic assessment must therefore be able to attest not only to what the student knows but to what they are capable of doing with that knowledge.


Aristotle is designed as a tool for applied competency assessment. It assesses students' ability to translate program learning into situated decisions, trade-offs, and actions.


Aristotle connects academic learning to workplace-relevant performance without reducing university evaluation to simple task simulation.

Function

Aristotle assesses the applied use of knowledge in constructed and supervised professional situations.

It supports analysis of:

  • the relevance of decisions taken in a given situation,
  • the coherence of the trade-offs made,
  • the ability to connect program concepts with field constraints.

The tool focuses on quality of applied reasoning within defined evaluation criteria.

Illustration: Simulating a crisis-management scenario for MBA students.

Operating Principle

Aristotle relies on a structured situational exercise, designed to explicitly assess identified competencies.


Responses are analyzed for:

  • expected theoretical frameworks,
  • decision frameworks used,
  • the coherence between diagnosis, action, and justification.

This approach makes it possible to assess competencies that cannot be captured by a classic academic paper, without sacrificing consistency or auditability.

Institutional Value

Applied Competency Assessment

Measurement of the ability to apply academic knowledge in structured professional contexts.

Decision and Trade-off Analysis

Assessment based on decisions and justification, not just declarative answers.

Academic-Professional Alignment

Explicit linking of program expectations with targeted operational competencies.

Aristotle enables institutions to document the professional relevance of degree-level competencies while preserving academic standards.
